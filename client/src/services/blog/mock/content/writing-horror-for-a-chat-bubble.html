A chat story has almost none of the tools a horror writer usually relies on. No long descriptions, no narrator, no slow build across a page. Just messages appearing one at a time on a phone screen. This post takes apart a short scene written for this article, beat by beat, to show how Horror Chat Stories gets its scares from timing.

The scene

Read it once at normal speed. The small grey labels show the pause before each bubble appears.

Unknown number · 2:47 AM

are you still awake

your kitchen light is on

how do you know that

turn it off. i want to see if you can

How an episode is shaped

Writing rules we follow

  1. The first bubble is the hook

    If it does not create a question, people swipe away.

  2. Write like people text

    Lowercase, short, occasionally wrong. Polished prose breaks the illusion of looking at someone's phone.

  3. Tune delays per message

    A quick exchange builds momentum; a long typing indicator builds dread. Small changes make a large difference.

  4. Let choices matter, a little

    Most choices change a few lines and the tone; a handful lead to different endings. Readers feel involved and the story keeps its shape.

Readers set the level

Dark mode makes late-night reading more immersive, and scare settings let people turn down sudden effects. More readers finish an episode when they control the intensity.
