Spanish from Mexico and Spanish from Spain are the same language, and speakers understand each other without trouble. But the accent, a few grammar habits and a lot of everyday words are different. If you are going to Mexico, or talking with Mexican family and friends, it pays to learn the Mexican version from the start.

The short answer

Learn Mexican Spanish if you will mostly hear Mexican Spanish. It is the variety spoken by the most people in the world, and it is the one you will hear across most of North America. Nothing you learn will stop you from being understood in Spain.

Pronunciation

  • The "th" sound. In most of Spain, z and c before e/i sound like English "th": gracias sounds like "grathias". In Mexico they sound like "s": "grasias".
  • The letter s. Mexicans usually pronounce every s clearly, which makes the accent one of the easiest to follow for learners.
  • The j sound. In Spain the j is rough and throaty. In Mexico it is softer, closer to an English "h".

Vosotros vs ustedes

This is the biggest grammar difference. In Spain, you talk to a group of friends with vosotros (¿Vosotros queréis comer?). In Mexico, everyone uses ustedes for any group, friends or strangers (¿Ustedes quieren comer?).

Good news: if you learn Mexican Spanish, you can skip a whole set of verb endings.

Past tense habits

For something that happened today, Spain tends to say He comido (I have eaten). Mexico usually says Comí (I ate). Both are correct; the Mexican habit is simpler for beginners.

Politeness

Mexican Spanish is famously polite. You will hear ¿Mande? instead of "¿Qué?" when someone did not catch what you said, plenty of por favor, and softeners like ¿Me regala...? or ¿Me da...? when ordering. Copying these habits makes you sound friendly, not just correct.
