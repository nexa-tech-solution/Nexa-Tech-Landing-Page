Ordering at a taquería is one of the best Spanish lessons you can get in Mexico: it is fast, it is friendly, and it follows the same pattern almost every time. Here is that pattern, the words on the menu, and a full example conversation you can practice before you go.

How a taquería order usually goes

  1. The taquero greets you, often with ¡Buenas! ¿Qué le doy? or ¿Qué te preparo? (What can I get you?).
  2. You say how many tacos and of what: Me da tres de pastor, por favor.
  3. They ask how you want them: ¿Con todo? (With everything?)
  4. You answer, maybe adding a drink.
  5. You pay at the end, often telling them what you ate.

"Con todo" and other toppings

Con todo usually means onion and cilantro. If you want something different:

  • Con todo, por favor. With everything, please.
  • Sin cebolla. No onion.
  • Sin cilantro. No cilantro.
  • Con queso. With cheese (sometimes costs extra).
  • ¿Cuál salsa pica menos? Which salsa is less spicy?

Salsas are usually on the counter or the table. If you are not sure, ask before you pour: green is not always milder than red.

Drinks

  • Un agua de horchata / de jamaica. A rice drink / hibiscus drink.
  • Un refresco. A soda.
  • Un agua natural. A plain water.

A full example

Practice it out loud first

The words are easy on paper. The hard part is answering quickly when someone asks ¿Con todo? with a line behind you. Practicing the exchange out loud a few times makes it automatic.
