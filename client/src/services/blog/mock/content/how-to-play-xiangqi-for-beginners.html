Xiangqi, often called Chinese chess, is played by hundreds of millions of people across China and Vietnam, where it is known as cờ tướng. If you already know Western chess, you will recognise the idea: checkmate the enemy general. The board, the pieces and a few unusual rules make it a different game, though. This guide covers everything you need for your first match.

In short
  • Pieces sit on the lines, not in the squares, on a 9 by 10 board split by a river.
  • Each side has a general, 2 advisors, 2 elephants, 2 horses, 2 chariots, 2 cannons and 5 soldiers.
  • You win by checkmating the general, and the two generals may never face each other on an open file.

The board

The board has 9 vertical lines and 10 horizontal lines, and pieces are placed on the points where they cross. A horizontal gap across the middle is the river. On each side, a 3 by 3 box marked with an X is the palace, where the general and advisors live. Red moves first.

How each piece moves

PieceMovesLimits
GeneralOne point up, down, left or rightMust stay in the palace
AdvisorOne point diagonallyMust stay in the palace
ElephantExactly two points diagonallyCannot cross the river; blocked if the point in between is occupied
HorseOne point straight, then one diagonally outwardBlocked if the first straight point is occupied
ChariotAny distance in a straight lineLike the rook in chess
CannonMoves like a chariotCaptures only by jumping over exactly one piece
SoldierOne point forwardAfter crossing the river it may also move sideways; it never moves back

Three rules that surprise chess players

  1. Flying general. The two generals may never stand on the same file with nothing between them. A move that opens that line is illegal, which makes the general a quiet attacking piece.
  2. Blocked horse. Unlike a knight, the horse can be stopped by a piece right next to it. Learning to spot a "hobbled" horse is one of the first skills to build.
  3. Stalemate loses. If you have no legal move, you lose. There is no draw by stalemate as in chess.

A simple opening plan

The most common first move is the central cannon: move a cannon to the middle file so it aims at the enemy general through the soldier in front of it. A good answer for the other side is to bring a horse out to defend the centre. After that, aim to:

  • Develop both horses early
  • Bring chariots to open files, since they are your strongest pieces
  • Push soldiers across the river only when they are supported
  • Keep advisors and elephants home to protect the general

Common beginner mistakes

  • Moving the chariots late. They do the most work and should join the game within the first ten moves.
  • Forgetting the cannon needs a screen. A cannon with no piece to jump over cannot capture.
  • Trading elephants and advisors early, which leaves the general open to horse and cannon attacks.
Practice with checkmate puzzles

Short puzzles where you must mate in one move teach piece patterns much faster than full games, because each one isolates a single idea.
