You do not need to be fluent to have a good trip to Mexico. A few dozen phrases, said out loud with confidence, cover most of what happens in a day: greeting people, ordering food, getting around, paying and asking for help. Here are 50 of them, grouped by situation, with the Mexican Spanish you will actually hear.

Greetings and basics

SpanishEnglish
Buenos díasGood morning
Buenas tardesGood afternoon (from about noon)
Buenas nochesGood evening / good night
¡Buenas!Hi! (casual, any time of day)
Por favorPlease
Gracias / Muchas graciasThank you / Thank you very much
De nadaYou're welcome
DisculpeExcuse me (to get attention)
PerdónSorry / pardon me
¿Mande?Sorry, what? (very Mexican)

¿Mande? is the polite way to ask someone to repeat themselves in Mexico. You will hear it constantly, and using it yourself sounds natural.

In Mexico a city bus is usually a camión, not an autobús. Small details like this are why it helps to practice the Spanish of the place you are going.

Reading the list is not the same as saying it

Most people who study phrase lists freeze the first time a real person answers back. The fix is to practice the phrases out loud, in the order they come up in a real conversation, before the trip. That is the idea behind Hablito: short real-life scenarios like getting a table, ordering tacos or taking a city bus, with a local Mexican voice that speaks first and you answering out loud.

Pick the five situations you are most nervous about, say each phrase out loud three times, and you will arrive ready for most of the conversations a trip throws at you.
