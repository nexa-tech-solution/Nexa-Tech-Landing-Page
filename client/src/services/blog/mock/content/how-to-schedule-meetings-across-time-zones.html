A meeting across three time zones looks simple until someone joins an hour early, or the calendar invite lands at 3 a.m. for half the team. Most of these mistakes come from the same handful of traps. Here is a practical way to find a time that works and to avoid the classic errors.

In short
  • Put every city side by side and look for the overlap of working hours.
  • Always check the actual date, because daylight saving time shifts on different days in different countries.
  • Write times with the city or zone attached, never just "3pm".

1. List the cities, not the zones

"EST" and "CET" are ambiguous and change with daylight saving. Start from the cities people are actually in, such as New York, London and Ho Chi Minh City. A city always maps to the right rules for its date.

2. Find the overlap of working hours

Line the cities up hour by hour and mark the hours between 9 a.m. and 6 p.m. for each one. The overlap is your shortlist. Some typical windows:

CitiesOverlapNote
New York + LondonMorning in New York, afternoon in LondonUsually 4 to 5 shared hours
London + SingaporeMorning in London, late afternoon in SingaporeShort, about 1 to 2 hours
San Francisco + Ho Chi Minh CityLate afternoon in SF, early morning in HCMCOften no overlap: someone stretches

When there is no overlap, rotate who takes the early or late slot so the same people do not always pay for it.

3. Check the date, not just the time

The US and Europe switch to and from daylight saving time on different weekends, and much of Asia does not use it at all. For a few weeks each spring and autumn, the gap between cities is an hour different from usual. A recurring meeting set in February can quietly move in March. Look up the specific date, not a rule of thumb.

4. Watch for the date line

A Monday 9 a.m. call in Sydney is still Sunday afternoon in Los Angeles. When cities are far apart, confirm the day as well as the hour, especially for deadlines.

5. Write times so nobody has to convert

  • Include the city: "10:00 London / 17:00 Ho Chi Minh City"
  • Use 24-hour time to avoid AM and PM mix-ups
  • Send a calendar invite, which converts automatically for each person
The most common mistake

Converting in your head with last month's offset. If a meeting is near the end of March or October, check again.
